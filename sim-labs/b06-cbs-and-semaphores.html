B06. Circular buffers and semaphores

Watch four CPUs move the pointers of two circular buffers, log every semaphore operation of the Dst handshake from inside the simulator, then write your own reader/compute/writer program, and break it three ways to see what ttsim and Watcher report.

Honesty banner. ttsim is a functional, bit-exact model of the chip. It is not a timing model. Every clock number on this page (the [4727] at the end of a run, the timestamps in the traces) counts ttsim's own steps, which the host drives. They are good for one thing: putting events in order within one run. They say nothing about how long anything takes on a real Wormhole or Blackhole. Where a result depends on timing (break B below), the simulator shows you one possible interleaving, and silicon may show another.

The question

A11 built the circular buffer from two counters and two private pointers. A12 showed that the compute kernel is three programs, and that they hand Dst back and forth with one 4-bit semaphore. Both chapters ran experiments: A11 printed the reader's write pointer, A12 printed the semaphore from each TRISC and broke the handshake by releasing twice.

This lab asks a sharper question. Take eltwise_binary, freeze it at the moment the reader pushes its fourth tile, and ask:

Where is every pointer, what does each of the four CPUs believe the counters say, and what value is the MATH_PACK semaphore at?

The CPUs will not agree. Each holds a private copy of part of the state, some of it is updated later by a Tensix instruction, and two of the four CPUs never initialise half the fields at all. By the end you will have watched all of that in a time-ordered trace, logged the semaphore from inside ttsim's model of the Sync Unit, written a 3-kernel program of your own that passes on Wormhole and Blackhole, and broken it on purpose in ways that ttsim, Watcher, or neither can catch.

Every command below was run in this orb against tt-metal 7aad8aac and ttsim b3620244. Every output block is pasted from those runs and trimmed with ….

1. Watch the CB pointers move

1a. Four views of two CBs

eltwise_binary streams 64 tiles: the reader (BRISC) fills c_0 and c_1, the compute kernel adds them into c_16, and the writer (NCRISC) drains c_16. Each CB has 2 pages of 2048 bytes. A11 already printed the reader's side. Here we add the compute side, and we print it from two TRISCs, because cb_wait_front/cb_pop_front compile only into TRISC0 (UNPACK) and cb_reserve_back/cb_push_back only into TRISC2 (PACK) (A12).

The patch b06-cb-dprint.patch adds DPRINTs to two overlay copies. In the reader it prints the write pointer and both stream-register counters of c_0 before each reserve and after each push. In the compute kernel the interesting additions are these:

Two details in it are already lessons. The << 4 is there because on a TRISC the CB pointers are not byte addresses: setup_local_cb_read_write_interfaces shifts them right by cb_addr_shift, which is CIRCULAR_BUFFER_COMPUTE_ADDR_SHIFT = 4 on TRISCs and 0 on BRISC/NCRISC (circular_buffer_interface.h:138-142, circular_buffer_constants.h:31). The unpacker and packer address L1 in 16-byte words, so the firmware stores pointers in their unit. UNPACK initialises read pointers only; PACK initialises write pointers and the page count only. The page count matters only to a producer, because only cb_reserve_back computes free space (fifo_num_pages − (received − acked)); a consumer just needs received − acked ≥ n. And TRISC1, the math core, has no cb_interface array in its binary at all. It never touches a CB, which is also why A12's math thread needs the semaphore.

Now the counters. TRISC0 prints ack(local) and ack(reg), and they agree here. They are not the same variable. In llk_pop_tiles, TRISC0 adds to its local tiles_acked immediately, but the stream register is written by a STOREREG that the TRISC pushes into its Tensix thread behind a STALLWAIT on the unpacker (A12 quoted it, llk_io_unpack.h:99-102). The wait loop compares the register received against the local acked:

tt-metal/tt_metal/hw/ckernels/wormhole_b0/metal/llk_io/llk_io_unpack.h:76-84 @7aad8aac

The packer mirrors it: cb_reserve_back on TRISC2 uses its local tiles_received and the register acked (llk_io_pack.h:32-43). Each side trusts its own local count of what it did and reads the register for what the other side did. Remember that uint16_t subtraction; break B will trip over it.

This log is not a timeline. Line order across BR, TR0 and TR2 is the order the host drained the print buffers (A12). Worse, DPRINT is slow and changes what it measures: R i=0 post-push … ack=1 says that by the time the reader had printed, compute had already consumed tile 0. The run took [13635] ttsim clocks against [7959] without the prints. (Reruns gave between 11,669 and 13,635: with prints on, the count also depends on how fast the host drains the buffers.)

1b. A flight recorder with a clock

To get a real timeline, record instead of print. Every RISC-V in a Tensix tile can read the wall clock register RISCV_DEBUG_REG_WALL_CLOCK_L (get_timestamp_32b() in risc_common.h:244-246), and ttsim returns its global clock there (ttsim/src/tile.cpp:1034), so timestamps from different cores are comparable. The patch b06-cb-recorder.patch stores (clock, pointer, received, acked) into a small array after each CB call and prints the array once, after the loop. It instruments all four CPUs for the first 8 tiles: reserve/push on BRISC (c_0), wait/pop on TRISC0 (c_0), reserve/push on TRISC2 (c_16) and wait/pop on NCRISC (c_16).

Five things to take from the trace:

  • The pointers really are private. BRISC's wr and TRISC0's rd for c_0 trace the same sequence 0x19e60, 0x1a660, 0x19e60, … but at different times. Neither ever reads the other.
  • The second page is used. At t=7030, BRISC is granted page 1 for tile 1 while tile 0 is still in page 0 (the acked it read is 0; in one of four reruns compute's pop landed in the same clock first and BRISC read 1), and at t=7103 the packer reserves page 0 of c_16 for tile 2 while the writer is still writing tile 1 from page 1.
  • A push on TRISC2 is not visible at once. At t=7030 TRISC2 has pushed tile 0 and its write pointer has moved, but the received register still reads 0. A few clocks later (1 to 6 across reruns) the writer reads 1. The register update is the STOREREG behind STALLWAIT(…, PACK) in llk_push_to_brisc:
tt-metal/tt_metal/hw/ckernels/wormhole_b0/metal/llk_io/llk_io_pack.h:60-70 @7aad8aac (comments trimmed)

  • The reader never got far ahead. At each of BRISC's reservations, received − acked was 0 or 1: at most one tile was waiting in c_0, so the second page was used for overlap and never for a backlog. (That is a statement about ttsim's interleaving, not about silicon.)
  • Counters end equal. After tile 7, both CBs read received = acked = 8: every page that was pushed was popped.
Each CB has one shared pair of counters and up to four private copies of its other state: a write pointer on the producer, a read pointer on the consumer, and local counter copies on the TRISCs. On the compute side, the shared counters are updated by Tensix instructions that wait for the unpacker or packer, so a TRISC's local count runs ahead of the register that other CPUs see. That lag is what makes the protocol safe: nobody is told a page is free or full before the hardware is done with it.

Remove the overlay when you are done (rm -rf $LAB_HOME/kernels/tt_metal), or every later run of eltwise_binary will print.

2. Trace the Dst semaphore inside ttsim

A12 printed MATH_PACK from the TRISCs and got 0 every time, because a TRISC's read is a snapshot of a coprocessor that may be far behind it. To see the semaphore as the hardware model sees it, log it where ttsim changes it.

Where the semaphores live in ttsim

The Sync Unit has eight 4-bit semaphores, each a Value and a Max (tt-isa-documentation WormholeB0/TensixTile/TensixCoprocessor/SyncUnit.md:31-41). ttsim keeps them in TensixState::sem[8] and sem_max[8], and, apart from the reset in tensix_init, touches them in six places: the four instructions in tensix.cpp, and the TRISCs' memory-mapped reads and writes of SemaphoreAccess[i] in tile.cpp. A write with bit 0 set is a get (decrement); with bit 0 clear it is a post (increment) (SyncUnit.md:58-80).

Returning false means "not executed yet": the instruction stays at the head of its thread and ttsim retries it on a later step. So a blocked SEMWAIT shows up as many failed attempts followed by one success. The patch logs the first failed attempt (SEMWAIT-BLOCK) and the success (SEMWAIT-PASS, with the number of tries and clocks spent), plus every SEMINIT, SEMPOST and SEMGET, plus MMIO posts and gets, plus MMIO reads whenever the value a TRISC sees changes (a polling loop would otherwise print thousands of identical lines). Each line has the clock, the thread (T0 unpack, T1 math, T2 pack), the semaphore, and its value before and after. Only one tile is logged, chosen by B06_TILE (default 0, which is logical core (0,0)).

The second surprise is the per-tile pattern. Look at clock 4523. MATH posts (value 1), the packer's wait passes, and before the packer's SEMGET, MATH's next acquire passes with value 1. Two more things in the full log are worth a look. First, sem5 (UNPACK_SYNC) has its own handshake, 64 pairs of T0 MMIO-POST sem5 0->1 followed by T0 SEMGET sem5 1->0: TRISC0's RISC-V posts through MMIO when it has written a set of unpacker addresses into a configuration context, and the unpack thread gets it back after the unpack MOP has run (llk_unpack_AB.h:264-280). The closest GPU idea is a pair of mbarriers guarding a two-stage shared-memory buffer between a producer warp and a consumer warp. Here the "barrier" is a counter with a ceiling, and the waits are not done by threads but by the coprocessor's wait gate, which holds back instructions the RISC-V has already issued. The TRISC that issued tile_regs_acquire has usually moved on long before its SEMWAIT passes.

3. Write it yourself

The reader

sim-labs/workbench/kernels/b06/reader.cpp:5-28
void kernel_main() {
    const uint32_t a_addr = get_arg_val<uint32_t>(0);   // DRAM address of A (runtime arg 0)
    const uint32_t b_addr = get_arg_val<uint32_t>(1);   // DRAM address of B (runtime arg 1)
    const uint32_t n_tiles = get_arg_val<uint32_t>(2);  // how many tiles to stream

    constexpr uint32_t cb_a = tt::CBIndex::c_0;
    constexpr uint32_t cb_b = tt::CBIndex::c_1;

    // Compile-time args 0.. describe A, then B; chain the offsets (A11).
    constexpr auto a_args = TensorAccessorArgs<0>();
    const auto a = TensorAccessor(a_args, a_addr);
    constexpr auto b_args = TensorAccessorArgs<a_args.next_compile_time_args_offset()>();
    const auto b = TensorAccessor(b_args, b_addr);

    for (uint32_t i = 0; i < n_tiles; i++) {
        cb_reserve_back(cb_a, 1);                        // block until c_0 has a free page
        cb_reserve_back(cb_b, 1);                        // ... and c_1 too
        noc_async_read_page(i, a, get_write_ptr(cb_a));  // start copying tile i of A into that page
        noc_async_read_page(i, b, get_write_ptr(cb_b));  // both reads are in flight together
        noc_async_read_barrier();                        // the bytes are in L1 now
        cb_push_back(cb_a, 1);                           // publish: received += 1, wr advances
        cb_push_back(cb_b, 1);
    }
}

The kernel is written in the classic free-function dialect the series uses (A11). The two TensorAccessors turn "page i" into a 64-bit NoC address; their compile-time blocks are chained with next_compile_time_args_offset() instead of a hard-coded 2. noc_async_read_page takes its size from the accessor's page size (dataflow_api.h:1241-1263). The order inside the loop is the whole protocol: reserve both, read both, one barrier for both reads, then push both. Pushing before the barrier would publish a page that may still be in flight; ttsim would not notice (B05).

The compute kernel

sim-labs/workbench/kernels/b06/compute.cpp:8-37
void kernel_main() {
    const uint32_t n_tiles = get_arg_val<uint32_t>(0);

    constexpr uint32_t cb_a = tt::CBIndex::c_0;
    constexpr uint32_t cb_b = tt::CBIndex::c_1;
    constexpr uint32_t cb_out = tt::CBIndex::c_16;

    compute_kernel_hw_startup(cb_a, cb_b, cb_out);  // UNPACK/MATH/PACK configure; MATH SEMINITs MATH_PACK
    add_init(cb_a, cb_b);                            // load the ELWADD unpack and math programs
    relu_tile_init();                                // MATH: set up the SFPU relu

    for (uint32_t i = 0; i < n_tiles; i++) {
        cb_wait_front(cb_a, 1);       // UNPACK: tile i of A is visible
        cb_wait_front(cb_b, 1);       // UNPACK: tile i of B is visible

        tile_regs_acquire();          // MATH: SEMWAIT until a Dst half is free
        add_tiles(cb_a, cb_b, 0, 0, 0);  // UNPACK: SrcA <- A, SrcB <- B;  MATH: Dst[0] = SrcA + SrcB (FPU)
        relu_tile(0);                 // MATH: Dst[0] = max(Dst[0], 0) on the SFPU
        tile_regs_commit();           // MATH: SEMPOST, the half belongs to PACK now

        cb_pop_front(cb_a, 1);        // UNPACK: free the input pages (acked += 1 once unpack is done)
        cb_pop_front(cb_b, 1);

        tile_regs_wait();             // PACK: SEMWAIT until MATH has committed
        cb_reserve_back(cb_out, 1);   // PACK: a free page in c_16
        pack_tile(0, cb_out);         // PACK: Dst[0] -> L1 page at c_16's write pointer
        tile_regs_release();          // PACK: ZEROACC the half, SEMGET
        cb_push_back(cb_out, 1);      // PACK: received += 1 once the packer is done
    }
}

Every comment names the TRISC that keeps the line (A12). Read the loop once per core:

  • TRISC0 sees: wait A, wait B, unpack A and B (the UNPACK half of add_tiles), pop A, pop B. The pops are safe right after the unpack is issued because the acked register is written only once the unpacker is done.
  • TRISC1 sees: acquire, ELWADD (the MATH half of add_tiles), the relu, commit. relu_tile exists only as MATH(SFPU_UNARY_CALL(…, _relu_min_, …, 0 /*threshold*/)), a "relu with lower limit 0" (relu.h:31-40), and its init relu_tile_init is MATH(SFPU_UNARY_INIT(relu_min)) (relu.h:15).
  • TRISC2 sees: wait, reserve, pack, release, push.

Within each core the order is what matters. On TRISC2, tile_regs_release must follow pack_tile (release zeroes the half it just packed), and on TRISC1 the relu must sit between acquire and commit. Across cores, the source order of, say, the pops and tile_regs_wait is irrelevant: they never share a binary. The Dst index is 0 every time, and yet consecutive tiles use different halves, because indices are relative to the half you currently own (A12).

The mirror image of the reader: wait, write, barrier, pop. The barrier before the pop matters on silicon, because popping tells the packer it may overwrite the page while the NoC might still be reading it (A11 discusses the cheaper noc_async_writes_flushed).

4. In the step 2 stock trace, why does the packer wait 40-52 clocks per tile but MATH never waits?

Because in ttsim, the slowest stage of this pipeline is upstream of MATH (unpack and the reader feeding it). MATH finishes a tile, posts, and its next acquire finds Value 1 < Max 2. The packer, downstream of MATH, finishes quickly and then has nothing to do until the next commit, so its tile_regs_wait blocks. The semaphore tells you which side of Dst is starved. With the slow writer the roles flip: MATH blocks 60 times and the packer's wait passes at once. Remember that these are ttsim step counts; on silicon the balance between stages can be different.

Recap

  • A CB's shared state is two stream registers, received and acked. Its other state is private: the producer's write pointer, the consumer's read pointer, and on TRISCs a local copy of the counter that core owns. On TRISCs the pointers are in 16-byte units.
  • TRISC0 initialises only read pointers, TRISC2 only write pointers and the page count, and TRISC1 has no CB interface at all (trisc.cc:79-93, 157-165).
  • On the compute side the shared counters are written by a STOREREG behind a STALLWAIT on the unpacker or packer, so they lag the TRISC's local count. A timestamped recorder shows it: TRISC2 had pushed while the register still read 0.
  • DPRINT perturbs timing and its line order is not time order. Record into an array with the wall clock, print at the end.
  • MATH_PACK starts life with Max 1 from BRISC's boot code; compute_kernel_hw_startup waits for 0 and re-initialises it to Max 2 (half sync) or 1 (full sync). Per tile: acquire = SEMWAIT on max, commit = SEMPOST, wait = SEMWAIT on zero, release = SEMGET. It reaches Max only when the packer falls behind.
  • Your own program is about 90 lines of host code and three ~25-line kernels, and it passed on ttsim WH and BH.
  • One pop too few: silent off-by-one data. A non-cumulative wait: a race whose wrong answer changes with every probe, made worse by a 16-bit wrap of received − acked. Waiting for more than the CB holds: a hang with Watcher's CRBW,CWFW signature. A 1-page CB: correct but serial, and a hang if any call needs 2 pages. Watcher caught none of the silent ones; LLK asserts caught a chunk that did not tile the ring.

Check yourself

TRISC0 printed pages=0 for c_0. Why does its cb_wait_front still work?

A consumer never needs the capacity. llk_wait_tiles only checks received − acked ≥ n. Free space, fifo_num_pages − (received − acked), is computed only by producers in cb_reserve_back, so only the write-side initialisation sets the page count, and TRISC0 does only the read side.

Right after cb_push_back on TRISC2, the received register still read 0. Bug?

No, it is the design. TRISC2 increments its local count at once but writes the register with a STOREREG that its Tensix thread executes only after a STALLWAIT for the packer (llk_io_pack.h:68-70). If the RISC-V store went first, the writer could start reading a page the packer had not finished writing. The writer saw 1 two ttsim clocks later.

In the stock semaphore trace, MATH's acquire passes while Value is 1. Is MATH writing the half the packer is reading?

No. With Max 2 there are two halves. Value 1 means one finished half is waiting for or being read by the packer, so the other half is free. The acquire hands MATH that other half (its offset flipped at commit, and the packer's flips at release). This overlap is the point of half sync; in full sync (Max 1) the same acquire blocks.

Break A did not hang. What change to the same line would make it hang, and what would Watcher show?

Stop popping for good, for example if (i < 2) cb_pop_front(cb_a, 1);. One missing pop leaves one page of a 2-page CB permanently used, which the ring survives; a second leaves none. The reader then blocks in cb_reserve_back on c_0 and stops feeding c_1 too, compute stalls waiting for c_1, and the writer waits for output. I ran it: timeout 30 killed it (exit 124), and under Watcher 59 consecutive dumps read CRBW,CWFW,UABD,MWDD, K, the same signature as B2.

Why did a single DPRINT make break B pass, and what does that tell you about testing on ttsim?

The bug is a race between TRISC0 reaching its second wait and BRISC pushing the second tile. A DPRINT costs TRISC0 many instructions, so BRISC always pushed first. ttsim is deterministic for one binary, but any change in instruction counts moves the interleaving: 1,681 wrong plain, 654 with a recorder, 5,145 under Watcher, 0 with a DPRINT. A pass on ttsim does not show that a protocol is right; read the waits and pops against the rules instead.

You need compute to consume 4 tiles of A per iteration. What CB depth and which waits?

At least 4 pages, and 8 if you want the reader to fill the next block while compute works on this one. Inside the iteration, either one cb_wait_front(cb_a, 4) or cumulative waits for 1, 2, 3, 4 if you want to start on early tiles, then one cb_pop_front(cb_a, 4). Make the depth a multiple of 4 so pops tile the ring.
